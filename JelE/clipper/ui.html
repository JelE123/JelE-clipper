Games

The clipper finds out by itself which game you play: the game in front on your PC, your Twitch category, your OBS capture or what you say. The editor, titles, stickers, words for the captions and hashtags then follow that game. You can always change it at the top of a stream or the editor.

Keys

Passwords and keys, grouped by what they are for. They are only kept on this PC (in config.js) and never shown or sent anywhere. Only the OBS password and your Twitch name are needed; the rest adds extras.

Finding moments

Captions

1ListenThe speech model writes down what you say, only from your mic track if it can find it.
2Fix wordsGame words are put right by themselves, plus your own words below.
3You checkIn the editor, words it is unsure about are marked. Press Tab to jump from one to the next. Viewers never see a "..." for what it did not understand.

Voicelines and music stay out best with your mic on its own audio track in OBS. Without a separate track I filter out music and known game lines (that list is in voicelines.txt, you can add lines to it).

Captions: words

Step 2 of the captions. Game words (agents, weapons, maps, Minecraft mobs and so on) are already built in for every game. Add here what only you say: names of teammates, your emotes, your channel. Words you change in the editor are learned by themselves. "Always fix" turns a word into another one every time. Voicelines are lines from the game that never belong in your captions.

Words I should know
Already built in: all Valorant agents, abilities and ultimates, weapons, maps, ranks, callouts, skins, pro teams and players, Twitch emotes and other big games. Mistakes like "kill joy" or "diffuse" are fixed by themselves.
